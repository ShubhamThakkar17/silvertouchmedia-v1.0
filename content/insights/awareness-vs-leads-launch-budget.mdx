Every launch budget faces the same tug of war. Sales wants enquiries from the first week. Marketing knows nobody enquires about a name they have never seen. Both are right, which is why the split matters more than the total.

### Start with the job, not the channel

Write down what the first three months must achieve. For most launches it is two things: enough people in the right area know the brand, and the ones who are ready can reach you easily. The first is awareness; the second is lead generation. They need different channels and different reports.

### A practical split

- **Weeks 1 to 6: weight towards awareness.** Hoardings on key routes, a strong print or radio burst, creators and city pages that people already follow. Keep search and WhatsApp live from day one so early interest is captured.
- **Weeks 7 onwards: shift towards leads.** Move money into search, lead-form ads and remarketing to everyone who saw the launch. Keep a smaller outdoor presence so the brand does not disappear.

The exact numbers depend on your category and city. A ₹10 lakh launch in one city looks very different from a ₹2 crore multi-state rollout, which is why our planner changes the number of channels with the budget and drops any channel that cannot reach its minimum effective spend.

### Measure each half on its own terms

Awareness should be judged on reach, frequency, branded search and recall. Leads should be judged on cost per qualified enquiry and how many become site visits or orders. Mixing the two, for example judging a hoarding on clicks, is how good launches get cut too early.

### Expect a slower start if you are new

New brands rarely see full payback in the first month. Awareness usually pays back over three to six months as people move from noticing you to calling you. Plan the budget and the board meeting with that in mind.
